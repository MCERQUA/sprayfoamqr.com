If you just scanned a QR sticker on a spray foam truck and landed on this site for the first time, here's a fast, honest guide to what you're looking at.

## The Page Itself

You're on a specific contractor's public profile — their business name, service area, years in business, and a stack of credential rows: license, insurance, and any certifications. Below that, photos of completed work, and their direct phone and email. Nothing on this page is generic marketing copy about the spray foam industry — it's specific to the truck you're standing next to.

## The Badge Next to Each Credential

Every credential row has a small badge on the right. Right now, as of this writing, every single badge on every profile in this directory says the same thing: "Documents provided by the contractor." That means the contractor told us this information themselves, and it has not yet been independently checked against an outside source like the state licensing board or the insurance carrier.

We're telling you that plainly instead of letting you assume otherwise. A badge that looked more official — a checkmark, a shield, a green "Verified" tag — without an actual check behind it would be worse than no badge at all, because it would let you trust something you shouldn't yet fully trust.

## What "Confirmed by CCA" Will Mean, Later

We're building toward actually checking these claims — calling the insurance carrier to confirm coverage is active, checking the license number against the state board's lookup tool, confirming a certification with the issuing body. When that happens for a specific claim, the badge on that specific row will change to "Confirmed by CCA" with the date we checked it. Until then, that badge doesn't exist anywhere on this site — not disabled, not greyed out, genuinely absent — because a partial or ambiguous verified-looking badge would defeat the entire point of this directory.

## What This Page Doesn't Tell You

This page tells you what the contractor claims about their license, insurance, and certifications. It doesn't tell you about their pricing, their availability, their specific reviews, or how good their actual spray technique is. This is a credentials check, not a full vetting service — a useful first filter before you talk to them, not a replacement for asking your own questions.

## If You Want the Actual Insurance Certificate

We deliberately don't publish the raw document — only the summary status line. If you're a general contractor or a homeowner who genuinely needs to see the actual certificate of insurance, ask the contractor directly. That's a normal, reasonable request, and a legitimate business should be able to provide it.
